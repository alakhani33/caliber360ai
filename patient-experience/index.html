Beyond Claims: Using Patient Experience Analytics to Identify Potential Healthcare Waste

Health-system case study · NERDS-5 supporting research

A large academic health-system analysis linked patients' most recent Net Promoter Score (NPS) with electronic health record utilization data. The study explored whether experience segments were associated with distinct patterns of healthcare use.

From insight to action

Three operational opportunities worth investigating.

Reactive care: Higher ED utilization among detractors suggests a useful starting point for investigating access, continuity and potentially avoidable acute-care pathways.

Capacity: Missed-appointment patterns can guide investigation of scheduling friction, outreach and unused appointment capacity.

Prevention: Differences in preventive-service engagement can help prioritize deeper analysis of gaps in routine care.

The strategic shift

Move from reporting patient experience as a standalone satisfaction metric to using it—alongside clinical, utilization and operational data—as an additional signal for targeted improvement.

Responsible patient-experience analytics

Survey responses are a sample—not the entire patient population.

In the case study, respondents differed significantly from the overall patient population by gender, race and age. Those differences matter: apparent experience patterns may reflect who answers surveys, not only differences in care.

Our analytical approach includes representation checks, stratification, weighting where appropriate, sensitivity testing and transparent communication of uncertainty.

Evidence with boundaries

The source work was an academic health-system case study prepared as supporting material for a CMS NERDS-5 application. It was not a state Medicaid implementation or a demonstration of quantified Medicaid savings. The broader methodology is adaptable, but results should not be assumed to transfer unchanged to other populations.
